Experience Lab: Eye Tracking & Object Detection

Combining traffic object detection in cycling footage with gaze and fixation data to support behavioural research.

Role

Data Scientist · Team project

Focus

Object detection & gaze analysis

Context

BUas Experience Lab

Tools & technologies

Python YOLO OpenCV Pandas Pupil Labs Streamlit Explainable AI

The problem

Experience Lab uses Pupil Labs glasses during cycling studies. The recordings contain video together with gaze and fixation data, but the eye tracking data alone does not show which traffic objects were around the cyclist or which of them they looked at.

Our goal was to reduce manual review by detecting relevant traffic objects, connecting them with the gaze and fixation data, and producing structured results for further analysis.

The same cycling frame before and after object detection, tracking and gaze and fixation matching.

How I approached it

01

04

Run the model on cycling footage, track traffic objects and connect them with gaze and fixation data.

What I worked on

I worked as one of the data scientists in a team of three. My work covered much of the process from understanding and preparing the data to modelling and connecting the results with the eye tracking data.

  • Explored and prepared Pupil Labs gaze, fixation and video data, including timestamp and data quality checks.
  • Built reusable preprocessing and data quality tools for both the eye tracking data and the object detection dataset.
  • Trained and compared YOLO models using different image resolutions and training settings.
  • Developed workflows for full video inference, automatic annotations, object tracking, gaze and fixation matching, and explainable AI.

From footage to research data

The workflow could take raw cycling footage through object detection and produce automatically annotated video together with CSV files containing detections and related metadata. The detections could then be tracked and connected with gaze and fixation data, giving researchers structured information about the traffic context around the cyclist and what they looked at.
